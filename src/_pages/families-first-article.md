Families First brings practitioners and agencies together to provide more connected help and protection for children and families. But bringing teams together does not automatically connect the different systems they use. Relevant information needs to be accessible, understandable and usable across organisational boundaries, without families repeatedly having to tell their story. 

Our data standards provide common definitions and structures for recording and exchanging information. They cover both the content of records—such as people, services, significant events, assessments and plans—and how systems make information available and exchange it securely. Our use cases illustrate how these standards could support Families First in practice. 

 

## Maintaining continuity through Family Help 

Family Help brings targeted early help and child in need support into a more connected offer. Practitioners from different agencies need to understand who is involved with a family, what support is already being provided and what has happened previously. This becomes particularly important when the lead practitioner and other team members use different recording systems.  

Our standards could help systems present this information consistently. Standards can help Family Help Lead practitioners identify relevant information from potentially long, complex case histories by enabling tools that find and synthesise case management system information, drawing on standards for people, professionals, organisations, services, service episodes, life events and assessments.  

Standards could also enable authorised agencies to easily share relevant information about people, professionals, organisations and services. This includes contributing data to single view systems. Implemented in local systems, these applications could reduce time spent searching across records and help practitioners build on existing knowledge rather than asking families to start again and repeat their story. More detail on this is outlined in our
[Multi-agency Enquiries](/UC09_multi_agency_enquiries) use case.

**What this could look like in practice:**

* A Family Help practitioner preparing to work with a family can identify current and previous service involvement and the professionals involved without manually searching many separate systems or contacting many services.
* A Family Help practitioner can easily identify related individuals for a child and associated plans / support needs.
* One coordinated Family Help plan can be updated by authorised practitioners across CSC and partner agencies.

## Strengthening multi-agency working: standards enable agencies to collaborate by inputting into a single record 

Multi-agency child protection depends on practitioners being able to bring together relevant information from different agencies and understand its significance for a child. Differences in how systems describe people, events, assessments and plans can make that information harder to exchange and use alongside the Family Help record. 

Our standards support multi-agency enquiries by providing a common structure for information around people, professionals, organisations, services, service episodes, life events, assessments and plans. This enables information to be more easily exchanged between systems, ensuring multi-agency teams have access to the key information they need to support someone. 

Our standards would also support multi-agency notifications, helping to provide consistent information about significant events, services and the people involved to notify relevant practitioners. These applications could support a more timely, informed response; they would not replace professional judgement or determine what action should be taken.

**What this could look like in practice:**

* A family receiving Family Help support has escalating needs that require a Child Protection response. The Family Help Lead can share a clear history and case file with the Lead Child Protection practitioner and multi-agency enquiries can be conducted in a timely way.
* Education, police or health services can contribute authorised information to the child’s record in a consistent format, while the source, date and context remain clear. 
* Relevant practitioners are notified when a significant event occurs or responsibility changes, supporting timely review and action without relying on informal emails or telephone calls.

## Strengthening family networks: standards enable families to view and input into appropriate parts of a record

Working with family networks means more than sharing information between professionals. Children and family members need opportunities to understand relevant information, contribute their perspectives and take part in decisions. Practitioners also need to understand the significant people and support already present in a child’s life.  

Our standards could enable add-ons that mean children and family members can view relevant information and contribute to agreed parts of their record, with appropriate permissions. This would draw on standards for people, professionals, organisations, services, service episodes, life events and assessments. 

This could support more informed participation in discussions about help and support. It is not, by itself, a family group decision-making process or a complete system for creating and managing family-led plans.

**What this could look like in practice:**

* A family group decision-making meeting records who took part, the relationships involved, the support each person has offered and the agreed actions, with these linked back to the child’s plan. 

* With appropriate permissions, a child or family member can view relevant information, add context or contribute to agreed parts of a shared plan.
