## Overview of the Session

### Referrals, requests and quality-of-care concerns

We shared our first draft standards for referrals, requests and quality-of-care concerns. 
Our objective is to reduce manual form-filling through machine-readable, interoperable and extensible data, while providing a reusable structure that can accommodate locally configured questions and workflows. 
These draft standards have now been published on our website. Please review the document and provide feedback on Github. 

#### What the working group discussed:
The group reviewed the proposed standard, providing several useful points for us to consider:

* __Scope and terminology:__ The discussion explored whether “referral” is always the right term for the first point of contact, and whether a single term can adequately cover safeguarding concerns, service requests and signposting. 
* __Responses from recipients:__  The group explored whether a referral, request or concern should support a response from its recipient. Feedback distinguished between confirmation of safe receipt, acknowledgement to the referrer, and later information about action or outcome. The group identified that confirmation of safe receipt is a useful tool, while flagging that information about outcome is sensitive and will depend on the type of referral. 
* __Minimum information about a subject:__ The group considered what information from the person standard should be mandatory, conditional or optional. Feedback mainly supported fields remaining optional given that in some situations only partial information is available. However, in different types of referrals it may be possible to make more information mandatory. The model should provide enough information for the recipient to identify or locate the subject and act, without blocking concerns simply because particular fields are unknown.  
* __Urgency and risk:__ The group discussed whether referrals and requests should include a level of urgency or risk. Feedback included support for local flexibility. However, the group flagged that immediate danger needs to be recognised in public-facing routes, so people can be directed to emergency services rather than relying on routine referral handling, supporting a single flag for urgency. The standard should avoid unnecessary additional layers of urgency or expecting referrers to make professional judgements that belong with the recipient.
* __Service catalogues and existing standards:__ We also discussed how services should be represented and linked to existing service catalogues. Feedback highlighted questions about local, regional and national services, geographical coverage, and the difference between the service requested and the endpoint to which information is routed. A minimal service object linked by an identifier to an external or local catalogue was discussed as one possible approach. 

### Validator Demonstration

The session included a demonstration of the validator we have built to test data against the machine-readable standards. 

You can find our validator [here](https://standard.socialcaredata.io/validator).

_N.b. On the public page we ask you to use test or anonymised records only. For real personal data, it is possible to download and use a command-line version of the tool or host the Validator on your own systems. 
Please raise GitHub issues where messages are unclear or apparently valid data does not pass validation._ 

Please raise GitHub [issues](https://github.com/SocialCareData/standard/issues) where messages are unclear or apparently valid data does not pass validation.
