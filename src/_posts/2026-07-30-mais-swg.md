## Overview of the Session

### Assessments and Plans

#### Assessments Standard 

Juliette led a walkthrough of our current work on the Assessments standard, which aims to enable the sharing of key information from Care Act assessments and care and support plans while preserving local authority autonomy and supporting broader applicability beyond adult social care. The model is not intended to standardise assessment practice or local workflows but to facilitate information sharing for care purposes. 

You provided us some key points to consider: 

* Eligibility is not purely determined by a person’s achievement of Care Act outcomes:
    * We changed some wording in the standards to make eligibility much more flexible to whatever processes govern it in different local authorities.
* In conducting a care act assessment or writing a care plan, a professional might note some findings about a person that would not fit into the fields of the assessment/plan itself.
    * We’ve made assessments and plans linked to serviceEpisodes in our data model, meaning that, when an assessment is conducted or a plan is written, professional observations about the person can also be made via serviceEpisode.finding.observation. Accordingly, we’re adding more types of observation (visit PR #54 and add your thoughts!)
* Similarly, some items wouldn’t sit in the assessments specification but would actually sit in our person standard, like communication needs.
    * We’ve added to our person specification to include things like aboutMe, communication needs, and language proficiency. 
* Positive statements (full-sentence answers to questions) generally provide greater context to readers of the data than single-words, especially if question data is not shared with answer data. 
    * We’re considering how we can ensure appropriate levels of context are shared when assessments data is shared. This might mean a small departure from FHIR’s Questionnaire pattern. 

#### Plans Standard 

Juliette then introduced the first iteration of the care and support plan standard. The care and support plan model includes core information about the person, practical details (who, what, when), and narrative elements about desired outcomes. The model aims to standardize practical information while allowing for some flexibility in narrative content. 

You provided us some key points to consider: 

* It’s important to know whether a care component of a plan is funded by direct payment. But it’s not important to know how much is being paid. 
    * We’ve added an element to careComponent to describe whether a component of a plan is funded via direct payment, and removed any references to overall prices or expenditure. 
* We might be too service-focused rather than person-centric. 
    * This naturally came about from our primary focus on standardising the administrative back-end behind care plans. We’re going to reassess this approach, though, and if necessary, we can be more person-centric by enabling Question-Answer pairs in the plans standard just as they are in the assessments standard. 
* Overall wishes for tracing document lineage and information governance 
    * We’ll be looking at metadata, provenance, and governance as part of our interoperability work, coming up. 

You can find our assessments and plans standards on the [website](/PUB05_assessments_and_plans_standard). Spot anything you think we’ve missed? Let us know by reporting an issue on GitHub. 

### Standards Roadmap and Future of the Working Group 

Finally, Ferry presented our current thinking on the standards implementation roadmap. We are now shifting our focus from developing standards towards piloting our standards. As we move into this new phase, the role of the Working Group will evolve, and we will revise the terms of reference to reflect this.
