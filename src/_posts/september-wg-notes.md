## Overview of the Session

### Referrals Standard

We’re starting work on a new set of standards to cover Referrals across children’s and adults’ social care. Neil led a walkthrough of our emerging thinking on the subject and presented the initial taxonomy we’ve created to identify the use cases that the standard should cover. He also led a discussion regarding our early thinking on what other standards and resources we would need to align with (such as Open Referral UK), concluding by referencing similarities in requirements between Referrals and the Assessments and Plans standard.  

You provided us with some useful points to consider: 

* Scope: You provided several additional types of referrals we need to consider in our taxonomy and highlighted the need to consider the more granular distinction between categories like referrals, escalations, signposting, and transitions. We also discussed modelling referrals as a linked chain of information spanning across agencies and professional teams.
* Information sources: Referrals come from a wide range of sources, not all of whom will be able to provide the same level of data quality. We need to account for this when drafting the standard.
* Reusability: We need to distinguish between what could form a generic, reusable referral message that can support different organisations and processes, and what could be an extension of this.
* Responses: We will explore whether we should include an optional response section as part of this standard to confirm acceptance of referrals.
* Alignment to clinical standards: We will need to ensure our standard is compatible with other referral requirements (for example clinical referral standards)

Thank you for your engagement and comments. Please add any further feedback to the board [here](https://miro.com/app/board/uXjVHkAxibs=/) (password: standards). We are now using your input to support development of our first sketch of the standard over the coming weeks.

### Standards Pilots

* Ferry then shared an update on our plans to pilot the standards.  The programme is moving from standards development into implementation, with pilots co-created by local authorities, case-management suppliers, and third-party integrations to test complete use cases and refine both standards and processes. The team is refining the programme’s 15 use cases so they address concrete problems in initiatives such as Families First and Neighbourhood Health rather than remaining overly agnostic. Pilots are expected to reveal new use cases, including possible AI-related applications, and may change the priority order.
* We are currently developing our pilot pipeline, with some areas now moving towards developing pilot briefs and several others under evaluation. The pilot pipeline will become a standing update in future working-group meetings, with the aim of sharing practical pilot results and common issues.
* If you are interested in engaging with the pilot process, or have additional use cases or local authority areas to propose for piloting, please reach out to the team.
