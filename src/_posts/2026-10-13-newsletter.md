## Standards Development Overview

Following a break in our newsletter updates, we wanted to share some of the progress being made to develop data standards that support better information sharing and data analysis across adult and children's social care. For those new to the newsletter you can read about why we are developing standards and the potential benefits they can unlock on our website.

Through extensive engagement with local authorities and suppliers in working group sessions, we have identified 15 priority use cases spanning adult and children's social care (see Figure 1). These describe practical scenarios where more consistent recording and information sharing could improve outcomes.

Each use case is supported by a set of standards that define both what information should be recorded and how it can be shared between systems. Together, these standards help ensure information is captured consistently and can be transferred more easily when needed. 

Visit the [use cases](/use_cases) and [standards](/publications) sections of the website to learn more.

## Preparing the next use cases for piloting

Building on the development of the programme's use cases and standards, we are now preparing for piloting with local authorities. Ten [use cases](/use_cases) covering social work recording and sensemaking, and multi-agency information sharing (MAIS), are ready for testing. The pilots will enable end-to-end testing across case management systems and other local authority applications, helping to validate that the use cases address real operational needs and deliver value for practitioners. They will also provide an opportunity to co-create with local authorities, with activity expected to begin in the coming months.

What the pilots will help us learn:

* How standards can be implemented across different systems and local contexts
* What changes to processes, technology or standards may be needed to support wider adoption
* What support local authorities and suppliers need to implement the standards at scale

Alongside preparations for piloting, work continues on the development of additional standards. The latest standard to be published is Assessments and Plans, now available on the programme [website](/publications). Further standards covering referrals and fostering are currently under consideration.

## From design to delivery: Placements standards in practice

The Children's Placements standards were developed to help local authorities build a clearer, more consistent picture of children’s placement sufficiency: what provision is needed, where, at what cost, and at what notice. By creating a shared data language across local authorities, the standards aim to support better regional analysis, commissioning decisions, and market-shaping.

We are now moving beyond design and into real operational settings. Implementation is complete in two regions (South West and North East), with work underway in two more (South East and Greater Manchester Combined Authority) and expected to finish in October 2026.

This practical work helps us learn how best to support local authorities with standards adoption.

**Building practical support for adoption**

Lessons from early adopters are informing an implementation playbook covering the practical steps, decisions and learning that can help future regions and local authorities implement the standards more quickly and consistently.  

We are also developing a maturity assessment model to help local authorities, suppliers and regional partners understand how fully the standards have been embedded across fields, workflows, reporting and decision-making, and where further support may be needed.

**Looking ahead**

The ambition is for all nine regions in the England to use the Placements standards by mid-2027 and we're delighted that DfE are using as the basis for the regional sufficiency analysis  to be done by the five new Regional Care cooperatives.

## Turning standards into practice: Exploring routes to standards assurance

Local authorities and suppliers have told us that standards need to be accompanied by a clear and transparent way of demonstrating how systems meet agreed requirements. We are therefore exploring a number of assurance mechanisms to support standards adoption.  

One of the models we are looking at is the Catalogue Agreement as a potential mechanism to support consistent adoption. This could include clear requirements, automated testing where relevant; a list of what software does and does not meet requirements; a list of model terms; and a procurement mechanism to wrap all of these together. This approach could make it easy for vendors to show that their software is standards compliant, and for buyers to require compliance in their procurements.

The Catalogue Agreement is already used in parts of the health and care technology market, including GP IT and the Digital Social Care Records (DSCRs) programme. We are drawing on learning from these approaches while considering what could work best for the adult and children's social care CMS market.

_Potential benefits of a Catalogue Agreement_

* Benefits for local authorities:
    * A clearer way to identify standards-compliant products
    * Greater confidence when considering technology solutions
* Benefits for suppliers
    * Clearer and more transparent requirements
    * A way to demonstrate how products meet standards

The Catalogue Agreement is one of several options under consideration. Over the coming months, we will work with CMS suppliers and local authorities to gather input on a range of potential approaches and help shape the way forward.
